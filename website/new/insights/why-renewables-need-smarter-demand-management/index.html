Why renewables need smarter demand management

Shifting when we use electricity can ease pressure on the grid and make it easier to use more wind and solar power.

By Adetoro AdetayoFebruary 10, 2025

On this page

  • Executive summary
  • Modernizing the grid
  • Market forces at work
  • Building better networks
  • Tomorrow’s blueprint

Executive summary

In a time where U.S. demand response programs are saving 29 GW during peak periods and engaging over 10 million customers, the energy sector is undergoing rapid change. This article explores how technological innovation, market dynamics, and policy frameworks are reshaping our approach to energy management and infrastructure development.

Modernizing the grid

Technological advancements, changing consumer behaviors, and the pressing need for sustainability are driving a major shift in the energy sector. At the heart of this change is demand response (DR), a concept that’s becoming increasingly crucial in addressing today’s energy challenges. In 2021, demand response programs in the United States showed remarkable potential, saving up to 29 gigawatts (GW) during peak periods. This involved over 10 million residential, commercial, and industrial customers, resulting in total energy savings of 1,154 gigawatt-hours (GWh).

Demand response refers to strategies and programs that encourage consumers to adjust their electricity usage during peak demand periods or in response to grid conditions. This collaborative approach between energy providers and consumers is essential for maintaining grid stability and reliability, especially as energy consumption patterns become more variable due to factors like electrification and the integration of renewable energy sources.

One of the most pressing issues in today’s energy systems is peak demand. This occurs when electricity consumption reaches its highest level within a specific timeframe, often leading to significant strain on the electrical grid. Such situations can trigger price spikes and necessitate the activation of less efficient and more polluting power plants, known as peaker plants, which are typically used to meet these surges in demand.

As energy consumption patterns evolve, shaped by increasing electrification in transportation and heating, alongside the growing deployment of intermittent renewable energy sources like solar and wind, the need for innovative solutions becomes more critical. The traditional model of centralized power generation is being challenged by decentralized energy resources, necessitating a shift toward smarter demand management strategies. These strategies include real-time monitoring, predictive analytics, and automated controls that allow for dynamic adjustments in energy use. Such innovations not only help flatten the demand curve but also facilitate the effective integration of renewable resources into the grid.

The urgency for effective demand management is clear. It’s essential for ensuring grid reliability in an era marked by fluctuating energy demands and an increasing share of renewables in the energy mix. Consumers can contribute to a more stable and efficient power grid by actively managing their consumption patterns, whether through financial incentives or automated systems.

Market forces at work

The transition to renewable energy is not just an environmental necessity; it’s becoming a strategic business move. Large commercial companies are recognizing the potential for enhanced operational efficiency and cost reduction through smarter demand management practices and renewable energy adoption.

Industry giants like Walmart, Amazon, and Microsoft are setting ambitious targets. Walmart aims to achieve 100% renewable energy by 2035, while Amazon has its sights on powering all operations with renewable energy by 2025. Microsoft’s pledge to be carbon-negative by 2030 includes a significant focus on renewable energy adoption.

These companies are leveraging advanced technologies to optimize their energy consumption patterns. Smart meters and analytics tools allow real-time monitoring of energy use, enabling adjustments during peak periods and exploitation of lower rates during off-peak times. This approach not only helps stabilize the grid but also reduces demand on fossil fuel-based power plants, contributing to a more sustainable energy landscape.

The shift toward renewables and efficient demand management requires expert guidance. Technical advisory services play a crucial role in this transition. They assess the feasibility of renewable energy projects and determine necessary infrastructure needs. These services provide critical insights into market conditions, regulatory frameworks, and technological advancements that can impact project viability. Firms like Ascend Analytics offer comprehensive economic valuations and carbon-free energy analyses, helping businesses navigate the complexities of renewable energy procurement.

The renewable energy market has the emergence of innovative marketplace platforms. These platforms have become vital tools for companies looking to procure renewable energy efficiently. Services like Renewable Exchange and Ascend Energy Exchange facilitate competitive bidding processes for power purchase agreements (PPAs). These platforms allow businesses to obtain bids from multiple developers for their renewable energy projects quickly.

The combination of ambitious corporate commitments, advanced technologies, expert advisory services, and innovative marketplace platforms is accelerating the transition to renewable energy. This shift is not only helping companies meet their sustainability goals but also optimizing their energy consumption and costs. The integration of demand management tools is transforming how investment firms and commercial developers approach sustainable energy projects. These advanced systems provide crucial insights into project technical details, enabling firms to assess feasibility and infrastructure needs before engaging potential customers.

Investment firms can evaluate various parameters such as energy consumption patterns, existing infrastructure capabilities, and potential upgrades needed for successful project implementation by leveraging the power of analytics and real-time data. Demand management systems analyze historical energy use data to identify trends and predict future demands, which is essential for crafting tailored solutions that align with client needs and sustainability objectives.

When assessing project qualifications, several factors play a crucial role in successful renewable energy deployment. Key elements include roof and electrical upgrades, building history, and customer creditworthiness. A building’s structural integrity may determine its suitability for panel installations, while the electrical system must be capable of integrating new technologies without compromising safety or efficiency. Understanding a customer’s creditworthiness is vital for securing financing options and ensuring project viability.

According to a report by KPMG, effective demand management practices can help firms prioritize projects based on their alignment with strategic goals and potential return on investment (ROI). This approach allows companies to focus their resources on initiatives with the greatest impact and financial benefits.

Energy executives are leveraging these tools to generate leads and streamline proposals for renewable energy projects. They can tailor their proposals to meet specific corporate sustainability goals by identifying potential clients actively seeking sustainable solutions.

The marketplace for renewable energy projects has evolved to support these efforts connecting developers with potential clients and enabling competitive bidding processes where multiple developers can submit proposals based on specific needs outlined by companies seeking renewable solutions. This drives down costs and encourages innovation among developers as they strive to offer the most attractive terms and technologies.

Building better networks

As the world transitions toward renewable energy sources, governments are taking on a crucial role in energy infrastructure planning and policy development. Their involvement is essential for establishing frameworks that support the deployment of renewable technologies while ensuring grid stability and sustainability. Governments facilitate necessary infrastructure upgrades and foster an environment conducive to innovation by formulating policies that incentivize renewable energy investments.

AI-driven algorithms predict future energy needs and optimize renewable energy deployment. These sophisticated tools analyze vast datasets, including historical energy consumption patterns, weather forecasts, and demographic trends. AI technologies can forecast solar and wind energy production by assessing meteorological data and historical performance metrics.

The impact of AI in the clean energy sector is substantial. According to a report by Kyoto Technology, the global market for AI in clean energy is projected to exceed $75.82 billion by 2030. This figure underscores the potential of these technologies in enhancing energy management and grid stability.

Governments are leveraging this data not only for predictive analytics but also to guide strategic investments in infrastructure that bolster grid reliability. Policymakers can identify areas for optimal renewable energy integration into existing grids by employing AI and big data analytics. Predictive maintenance models have the potential to inform governments about when and where upgrades are necessary. Such proactive approaches to infrastructure management are crucial as the energy landscape becomes increasingly complex. AI’s capabilities extend beyond forecasting to enable real-time adjustments in energy distribution based on current demand and supply conditions. 

Demand response (DR) systems have emerged as crucial tools for maintaining grid stability and managing energy consumption during peak periods. These systems enable consumers to adjust their energy usage in real-time based on supply and demand fluctuations, contributing to a more resilient electrical grid. In using technologies like smart meters and automated controls, DR programs allow utilities or grid operators to send alerts during critical times when energy demand is expected to exceed supply.

The impact of demand response on reducing peak energy demand is significant. During high consumption periods, grid operators face challenges in balancing supply and demand. DR programs help mitigate these challenges by incentivizing consumers to curtail their energy usage. Studies have shown that even a small 1% reduction in peak demand can lead to significant savings at the system level, resulting in billions of dollars in avoided costs for both utilities and consumers. 

DR strategies also offer significant environmental benefits by minimizing reliance on backup fossil-fuel-based power during peak times. Traditional backup power sources, often called “peaker plants,” are typically less efficient and more polluting than renewable energy sources. Utilities can decrease the need for fossil fuel plants by reducing peak demand through effective DR strategies, thereby lowering greenhouse gas emissions and supporting climate change mitigation efforts. Furthermore, demand response enhances the overall efficiency of energy systems by promoting the integration of renewable energy sources. DR can facilitate integration by encouraging consumers to use energy when renewable generation is high, thus optimizing the use of clean energy.

Tomorrow’s blueprint

Smarter demand management is essential for businesses, developers, and governments as they transition toward cleaner energy. It enables companies to better align their sustainability goals with practical energy solutions while improving efficiency and cost savings. Developers benefit from streamlined processes that help them deliver tailored energy projects that meet both environmental and financial objectives. Governments, in turn, can use these tools to enhance infrastructure planning and policy-making to support renewable energy integration.

Collaboration between all stakeholders is key to creating a more resilient, cost-effective, and sustainable energy system. In taking shared responsibility for optimizing energy use and reducing peak demand, we can collectively move toward a cleaner and more reliable energy future. Demand response will play an important role in this process, helping to balance energy consumption and ensure that everyone benefits from a stable and sustainable power grid.
